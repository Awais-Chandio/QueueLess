# 1A.3 RLS on organization membership

## Goal
Every policy and RPC caller check that grants clinic-side access goes through organization membership.
Tenant isolation is the top priority.

## How access is decided now
| Helper | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `private.is_center_staff(center)` | **owner** of the center's organization, or **receptionist** of it **and** assigned to that center in `staff_centers` (center scope inside an org) |
| `private.is_doctor_self(doctor)` | `doctors.profile_id = caller` **and** a **doctor** member of that doctor's organization |
| `private.staff_can_view_profile(profile)` | the patient has an appointment at a center the caller is staff of |
| `can_manage_center`, `can_manage_doctor`, `can_operate_appointment`, `can_view_appointment` | unchanged; they build on the two above, so every RPC using them follows (call/confirm/start, snapshots, breaks, averages, `refresh_live_queue`, …) |
| `public.is_admin()` | platform admin, unchanged |

All of them go through `has_org_role`, so a **suspended organization loses all clinic access at once**.

## Policies rewritten
- Inline `doctor_id IN (SELECT … doctors WHERE profile_id = auth.uid())` → `is_doctor_self()`: appointments
  (read/update), doctor_leaves, doctor_queue_settings, doctor_schedules, queue_updates, visit_summaries (3),
  prescription_items (4). Also moved from `PUBLIC` to `authenticated`.
- JWT `app_role` claim checks → `is_admin()`: appointments admin update; the duplicate "admin manage doctors"
  policy dropped ("… by profile role" already covers it).
- `profiles.role` subquery → `is_admin()`: service_centers admin policy.
- `staff read doctors at own center`, `staff toggle break` → `is_center_staff()`.
- RPCs: `get_center_queue_snapshot` and `complete_appointment` matched the doctor inline; now `is_doctor_self()`.

## Isolation fixes found on the way
1. **`center_daily_tokens` was readable by every signed-in user** (`USING (true)`): any account could read every
   clinic's daily patient volume. Now `can_manage_center(center_id)`. The app never reads this table.
2. **Patients could rewrite `center_id` / `doctor_id` / `token_number` on their own appointment** (table-wide
   UPDATE grant + a policy that only checked the new status), i.e. push a row into another clinic's queue or
   jump the queue. Clients now have **column-level UPDATE** on exactly what the app writes: status, its
   timestamps, cancel fields, `notes`, `scheduled_at` (reschedule) and a few legacy display columns.
   `INSERT/DELETE` also revoked from `anon` (no policy allowed them anyway).

## Decisions (safest option)
- **Owners get the operational access staff have, across their organization.** No new catalog write powers
  (doctors, services, schedules stay platform-admin writes), so a clinic owner can't add doctors or services yet.
  That arrives with onboarding in Phase 1B.
- **Left as is, on purpose:** public reads the patient app needs (`service_centers`, `services`, active `doctors`,
  `doctor_schedules`, `doctor_leaves`, `doctor_services`), and signed-in reads of `doctor_queue_settings` /
  `center_queue_settings` (current token, average time and breaks are shown to waiting patients). None of these
  hold patient data.
- Policies still matching `profile_id = auth.uid()` are *own-row* checks (a doctor's own record, own
  `staff_centers` rows, own avatar folder), not clinic access.
